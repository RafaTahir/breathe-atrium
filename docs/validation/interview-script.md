# BreatheAtrium Customer Validation Interview Script

Use this kit for fast, practical validation before the pitch. The goal is not to sell. The goal is to learn whether overheated atriums are painful enough, measurable enough, and fundable enough to justify a pilot.

## Interview Setup

- Keep each interview to 10-15 minutes.
- Ask permission to capture anonymous quotes.
- Do not pitch too early. Start with their current experience.
- Mark all findings as interview evidence, not measured building performance.
- End by asking what proof they would need before approving a pilot.

## Facility Manager Questions

1. Which atrium or large glazed space in your building creates the most comfort complaints?
2. When is the heat or discomfort worst: morning, midday, afternoon, evening, or during events?
3. What do occupants, tenants, or staff usually complain about: heat, humidity, stale air, glare, smell, or something else?
4. What are you doing today to manage the problem?
5. What does the current workaround cost you in energy, staff time, complaints, or operational complexity?
6. Who would need to approve a 90-day pilot in this building?
7. What data would make you trust that an atrium cooling retrofit is working?
8. Which success metric matters most: occupied-zone temperature, comfort hours, airflow, CO2, HVAC runtime, complaints, or energy proxy?
9. What would make you reject a pilot: disruption, fire/smoke concerns, humidity, maintenance, capex, tenant risk, or weak evidence?
10. If a low-disruption pilot could be installed with sensors and a verification report, how willing would you be to trial it?

## Atrium User / Student / Staff Questions

1. How often do you spend time in the atrium or nearby spaces?
2. How comfortable does the atrium feel during a typical hot day?
3. What is the worst time of day to be there?
4. What do you notice most: heat from above, stagnant air, humidity, glare, noise, smell, or crowding?
5. Do you avoid the atrium, leave quickly, change where you sit, or use fans/other workarounds?
6. What would make the atrium feel noticeably better?
7. If the building tested a passive cooling retrofit, what improvement would you actually notice?
8. Can we use an anonymous quote from your experience in our validation summary?

## Architect / MEP / HVAC Questions

1. In your experience, what causes atriums in tropical buildings to underperform on comfort?
2. What are the most common current solutions: more cooling, vents, fans, shading, smoke exhaust use, green features, or redesign?
3. What technical constraints would determine whether solar-assisted natural ventilation is viable?
4. What would you need to see in a 90-day pilot before recommending a full retrofit?
5. Where should BreatheAtrium integrate with BMS, dampers, HVAC coordination, fire/smoke strategy, and maintenance workflows?
6. What would make this attractive to your clients: lower cooling burden, comfort evidence, ESG narrative, retrofit practicality, or dashboard reporting?

## Evidence To Capture

- Pain score from 1 to 5.
- Current workaround.
- Worst time of day.
- Decision maker or approval path.
- Strong direct quote.
- Willingness to pilot.
- Any risk or objection that would block adoption.
