# BreatheAtrium Judge Defense Sheet

Use this for fast Q&A rehearsal. Answer with confidence, then return to the core message: mixed-mode atrium comfort, measured pilot evidence, and a practical retrofit business.

## 1. Does this replace air-conditioning?

No. BreatheAtrium is a mixed-mode atrium comfort retrofit. It reduces heat build-up and cooling burden where site conditions support it, while HVAC remains available for humidity control, peak loads, and comfort assurance. We are not claiming full air-conditioning replacement.

## 2. Will this work in humid Malaysia?

Malaysia is exactly why the system must be measured. The passive exhaust path can still help remove hot air, but humidity control is site-dependent. The pilot tracks humidity, CO2, temperature, airflow, and HVAC coordination before we claim savings. Optional water-wall features are only used if humidity risk is acceptable.

## 3. What makes the UM IP essential?

The UM IP gives the passive cooling engine: solar-assisted natural ventilation for atriums. BreatheAtrium turns that into a commercial system with intake/exhaust design, sensors, BMS integration, dashboard evidence, and a pilot workflow. The IP is the technical anchor; the product is the deployable retrofit package.

## 4. What if the patent status is complicated?

Then we handle it professionally. We would confirm ownership, prosecution status, claims scope, and licensing route with UM before making exclusivity claims. If licensing needs time, the business can still pilot around design services, measurement, and dashboard tooling while the IP route is clarified.

## 5. How do you prevent rain ingress?

Through normal building-services design: louvers, baffles, rain hoods, drainage, damper control, and site-specific detailing. Rain protection is part of the audit and retrofit design, not an afterthought. We would not install open paths without weather protection and facilities approval.

## 6. What about fire and smoke control?

Fire and smoke strategy is a hard requirement. BreatheAtrium must coordinate with existing smoke exhaust, dampers, fire alarms, and code requirements. The pilot would not override life-safety systems. Any BMS integration must fail safe and be reviewed with qualified MEP/fire-safety professionals.

## 7. What about mold and water-wall maintenance?

The water wall is optional, not core. If used, it needs water quality control, maintenance access, humidity monitoring, and shutoff rules. If the site has mold or humidity risk, we keep the first retrofit focused on airflow, exhaust, sensors, and controls.

## 8. Who pays?

The economic buyer is the building owner, REIT, campus administration, hotel owner, mall operator, or government facilities budget holder. The user is the facility team. The first paid step is an audit, then a 90-day pilot, then a retrofit and monitoring subscription if evidence supports scaling.

## 9. What is the first pilot site?

Our first target is a UM-linked or institutional atrium where access, researcher support, and facilities collaboration are realistic. The ideal first site has visible heat discomfort, an engaged facility manager, and permission to install sensors for baseline and post-install verification.

## 10. What is the business model?

Audit, pilot, retrofit, monitoring. We start with a paid atrium audit, convert suitable sites into a 90-day pilot, sell the full retrofit if results justify it, and keep recurring revenue through dashboard monitoring and seasonal optimization. Later, certified partners can extend delivery.

## 11. Why not just install more AC?

More AC can cool symptoms, but it can increase energy burden and still leave stratification or stagnant air. BreatheAtrium attacks the atrium physics first: remove trapped hot air and pull replacement air across occupied zones, then coordinate with HVAC instead of making HVAC work alone.

## 12. Why not generic vents?

Generic vents are components. BreatheAtrium is a measured system: intake strategy, high-level exhaust, solar-assisted stack path, optional assist, sensors, BMS logic, dashboard, and pilot report. The difference is not just airflow; it is evidence and operational integration.

## 13. How do you measure success?

Pilot targets include lower temperature stratification, better occupied-zone comfort, improved airflow trend, acceptable humidity, stable CO2, lower cooling-energy proxy, and positive facility/user feedback. We compare baseline and post-install periods with weather context before making claims.

## 14. What are the biggest risks?

Site variability, humidity, rain detailing, fire/smoke coordination, maintenance burden, and weak measured impact. That is why we lead with audit and pilot rather than selling a guaranteed retrofit. The product is designed to surface risks early.

## 15. What is scalable here?

The repeatable system: audit method, sensor plan, design rules, BMS integration checklist, dashboard, pilot report, ROI calculator, and certified partner playbook. Atriums differ, but the workflow can scale through MEP, facade, ESCO, and BMS partners.

## 16. How will you partner with MEP/ESCO/facade firms?

We give partners a packaged offer: audit template, design toolkit, sensor bundle, dashboard, commissioning checklist, and verification report format. MEP firms bring building-services credibility, ESCOs bring energy framing, facade firms handle envelope constraints, and BMS partners handle controls.

## 17. What can you build in 90 days?

A real pilot package: baseline sensor mapping, a prototype airflow path or light retrofit, sensor stream, dashboard, BMS coordination assumptions, monitored operation, and a verification report. Full construction may be phased, but a credible evidence pilot is achievable.

## 18. What is the expected payback?

Payback is site-dependent and should not be guessed. Our ROI calculator uses assumptions and ranges: current cooling cost, atrium share, expected reduction range, pilot cost, retrofit cost, and monitoring fee. The pilot turns those assumptions into a measured investment case.

## 19. What data do you need before installation?

Atrium geometry, roof/glazing conditions, existing HVAC and BMS setup, smoke control strategy, rain exposure, occupancy patterns, complaints, temperature stratification, humidity, CO2, airflow proxy, and cooling-energy or HVAC runtime proxy. Without baseline data, we do not claim savings.

## 20. What is the minimum viable retrofit?

Sensors, baseline dashboard, low-level intake path, high-level exhaust path, and a controlled operating protocol. Solar-assisted chimney enhancement and optional water wall can be added after site assessment. The minimum viable retrofit must be reversible, measurable, and safe.

## 21. What does the dashboard actually do?

It shows live or simulated sensor readings, compares dead-atrium and BreatheAtrium modes, explains the airflow concept, calculates ROI assumptions, structures the 90-day pilot report, and gives facility teams evidence for a commercial decision. It is a demo tool and future monitoring interface.

## 22. How does the solar chimney work?

Solar heat warms the chimney or upper exhaust path. Warm air rises and exits high, creating a pull that draws replacement air through low-level inlets and across the occupied zone. The sun becomes part of the exhaust driver instead of only adding heat load.

## 23. What happens at night or during rain?

At night, the system can shift to night purge or passive ventilation if outdoor conditions help. During rain, dampers and rain-protected openings manage exposure. Operation is site-dependent and should coordinate with BMS rules, humidity limits, and facility preferences.

## 24. How do you avoid fighting the HVAC?

Through BMS-aware control logic. BreatheAtrium should inform damper positions, ventilation modes, alerts, and HVAC coordination rather than operating blindly. The goal is passive-first support with HVAC in reserve, not two systems competing.

## 25. What is your ask from UM/accelerator?

We need IP commercialization guidance, a pilot atrium, researcher support, facilities access, permission to install sensors, and introductions to MEP/BMS/ESCO partners. The accelerator can help turn a strong technical concept into a validated pilot and commercial pathway.

## Three Lines To Repeat Under Pressure

1. BreatheAtrium does not replace all air-conditioning; it reduces atrium heat build-up and cooling burden as a mixed-mode retrofit.
2. We do not claim savings without measurement; the 90-day pilot converts assumptions into evidence.
3. The scalable product is the package: UM IP, passive airflow design, sensors, BMS coordination, dashboard, pilot report, and partner delivery.
