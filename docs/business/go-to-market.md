# BreatheAtrium Go-To-Market

BreatheAtrium should enter the market through trust, evidence, and visible reference sites. The first customers should not be asked to buy a broad platform. They should be asked to approve a focused 90-day pilot for a known atrium heat problem.

## Beachhead

The beachhead is UM, campus buildings, and government or institutional atriums where access, research support, and facilities collaboration are realistic.

These sites are ideal for early proof because they combine visible public spaces, operational pressure, sustainability interest, and a credible path to measured validation. The first win should become a reference case, not just a prototype demo.

## Phase 1: Pilot Proof

Secure one instrumented pilot atrium. Run baseline measurement, install the prototype or light retrofit, operate for 90 days, and produce a verification report.

Pilot targets should include reduced heat stratification, improved occupied-zone comfort hours, better airflow trend, acceptable humidity behavior, and a cooling-energy proxy. The goal is not to overclaim savings. The goal is to earn the right to propose a full retrofit.

## Phase 2: Institutional Buildings

After the first proof point, expand to universities, government offices, libraries, hospitals, and civic buildings. These buyers value public comfort, ESG credibility, and transparent measurement.

The sales message: improve atrium usability and reduce cooling burden with a mixed-mode retrofit that can be audited, piloted, and verified.

## Phase 3: Malls, Hotels, and Offices

Move into commercial atriums once the pilot evidence and installation playbook are strong. Malls and hotels care about visitor comfort and brand experience. Office towers and mixed-use buildings care about tenant experience, operating cost pressure, and asset differentiation.

This phase should use case studies, before/after dashboards, and facility-manager testimonials rather than broad market claims.

## Phase 4: ESCO, MEP, Facade, and BMS Partner Network

Scale through partners that already sell into building owners. ESCOs bring energy-performance framing. MEP consultants bring design credibility. Facade specialists understand envelope constraints. BMS partners enable mixed-mode control and monitoring.

The partner offer should include audit templates, design rules, sensor packages, dashboard setup, commissioning guidance, and certification.

## Sales Motion

The sales motion is:

```text
audit -> pilot -> retrofit -> monitoring
```

The audit qualifies the site. The pilot creates evidence. The retrofit captures project revenue. Monitoring turns the installation into recurring value and keeps the facilities team engaged.

## Key Partnerships

Priority partnerships:

- UM researchers and IP commercialization stakeholders.
- Facilities teams with candidate pilot atriums.
- MEP and building-services consultants.
- BMS and controls integrators.
- Facade and ventilation contractors.
- ESCOs and green-building advisors.
- Public-sector innovation or sustainability programs.

The core go-to-market discipline is simple: win one credible pilot, measure it well, package the evidence, and repeat through partners.
