Executive pilot proposal

90-Day BreatheAtrium Pilot

A concise pilot path for facility managers and building owners with overheated atriums, using solar-assisted natural ventilation as a mixed-mode comfort and cooling-burden retrofit.

Target customer

Facility teams responsible for hot atriums

Facility managers and building owners operating malls, universities, government offices, hotels, office towers, or transport hubs with glass atriums that overheat during occupied periods.

Problem

Heat build-up becomes an operating burden

Hot upper layers, occupant discomfort, weak air movement, and higher cooling burden can make atriums expensive to operate and difficult to keep comfortable.

Proposed intervention

Passive-first retrofit, measured before claims

Audit, sensor mapping, solar-assisted ventilation design, low-level intake, high-level exhaust, optional water-wall or evaporative assist, and BMS integration for mixed-mode operation.

Pilot scope

Twelve weeks from baseline to scale decision

Week 1-2
Audit and baseline measurement

Site walk-through, heat-risk mapping, sensor placement plan, and pre-install temperature, humidity, CO2, airflow, and cooling-energy proxy baseline.

Week 3-6
Prototype or retrofit installation

Low-level intake path, high-level exhaust path, solar-assisted ventilation design, optional water-wall assessment, and BMS coordination.

Week 7-10
Monitored operation

Operate the passive-first mode, watch comfort indicators, tune control assumptions, and keep a facilities log of operational observations.

Week 11-12
Verification and scale decision

Compare baseline and operating periods, summarize findings, document constraints, and decide whether to scale, redesign, or stop.
